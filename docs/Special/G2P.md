# G2P

Genotype-to-phenotype prediction for bacteria: eight binary phenotypes for 13,669
strains, each represented by the 2,047 Pfam protein-domain families present in its
genome, from the BacDive-AI release. Every trait is registered twice —
`bacdive_<trait>` uses a random split, `bacdive_clade_<trait>` holds out whole
gene-content clades, since strains of one species share ~99% of their gene content
and a random split places near-duplicates on both sides. Accuracy is a weak summary
at these prevalences (2.6%–55%), so the tables below report macro-F1. Note that
`Aerobic` and `Anaerobic` mean *obligate* and are mutually exclusive; facultative
organisms are negative for both.

Five entries, all on identical splits: `g2p_multitask` (shared 512/256 trunk, one
head per trait, masked loss), `g2p_mlp` (per-trait MLP with the same hidden sizes —
the control that isolates cross-trait sharing from simply using a neural network),
`g2p_gbm`, `g2p_logistic` and `g2p_rf` (the architecture of the published BacDive-AI
models). Bold marks the best macro-F1 per row.

Reference: [BacDive-AI](https://github.com/JKoblitz/bacdive-AI) ·
[BacDive](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkae959) ·
[Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.13757323)

Gradient boosting is the strongest single model under clade holdout (0.8498);
multi-task sharing is third (0.8432) and is matched by the per-trait MLP (0.8450),
so on aggregate the sharing buys little over using a neural network at all. Its
advantage is confined to the starved traits: acidophily has 70 positives in the
whole dataset, and under clade holdout `g2p_multitask` reaches 0.594 where the MLP
and random forest sit at majority-class performance (0.494).
